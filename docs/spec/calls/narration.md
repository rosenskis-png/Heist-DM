# Narration call

**Job:** prose only. Narrate the settled results of execution and the world call: the montage, any stop, the world's response.
**Runtime:** `sample.json`, replay off, `default` (D32).
**Sees:** the "Narration" column of `docs/spec/context.md` §2, including the companions' voice notes for brief lines inside the montage.

## Returns (`schemas/calls/narration.js`)
- The narration.
- **Texture** it introduced, returned separately as canon. It may add texture, never state (principle 3).

## Paint the scene (D49)
The narration gives the player a picture they can hold: who is where, what they're doing, what draws the eye, the rhythm of the place. It doesn't survey it in feet and lists. Exact layout lives in the map, and any detail the prose leaves out is still there for the player to ask about in Moment. The same goes for the opening description.

## Interruptions and hidden things
- **An interruption gets its moment** (D36, D46). The narration doesn't just stop. It lands on the instant someone in the party recognizes that things aren't as planned, and gives that moment room: a few sentences of what they see and hear, how it lands on them, and where everyone is now ("Fern is halfway to the bars when the club stops rattling. Snag lifts the ring off the peg, weighs it, and clips it to his belt. The peg hangs empty in the torchlight, and he starts down the corridor."). For an arrival, how much warning the party had shapes the beat: footsteps on the stair, or a door already swinging open. It ends there, at a moment the player can plan from.
- **Hidden things stay out of the prose** unless the results reveal them at that moment. This call sees the whole packet so it never contradicts the hidden parts, not so it can describe them.

The companions' own conversation happens in their calls, not here (`docs/spec/companions.md` §7).
