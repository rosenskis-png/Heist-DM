# Act call

An act is beginning. You look at what came before and what lies ahead, and decide what should come up during this act: its setting in general, its story, the clues it holds, what it brings for each companion, the places its calm rooms are set in, and who stands at its end. You decide from what the party has actually done, so the act answers their choices.

You don't design any room. Rooms are written later, one at a time, by someone who sees the act's setting, question and aim, and only the one or two things from your lists that code hands that room. So keep what every room reads general, and put anything particular in a list, where it reaches one room only.

## What you write
- **goal:** the act's job, given to you, in this world's terms. Only the climax reads it.
- **setting:** this part of the place in general, in a sentence or two: what it is and who's in it. Never particular rooms, objects or sights: those are each room's to invent.
- **question:** what this act is about, as a question the act answers. One sentence, about the party's situation.
- **aim:** what the party should come away knowing or holding by the act's end. One sentence.
- **state:** what has changed in the region because of what came before. Only the climax and the next act read it.
- **holders:** the ids of the factions that hold the region now. Start from the region's holders, and change them if what happened would.
- **threads:** for each companion whose story should come up this act, her slot and a line on what of it comes up: a step on her quest when its act is near, a feature her story needs, something that would test what she believes. None for a companion whose story rests this act.
- **settings:** for each calm kind of room (`quiet`, `oddity`, `campfire`), two or three places that could only exist in this region: strange, beautiful, funny or haunting, and different from each other. A quiet room is a breath; an oddity is something wondrous or strange with a story; a campfire is somewhere safe and memorable to sleep.
- **news:** what the party might hear or see of what earlier acts set in motion, each a line on its own. None if it's Act 1.
- **mysteries:** the ids of one or two of the world's mysteries whose journal pages this act's rooms may hold: the ones closest to this act's question.
- **returning:** names, exactly as given, of people the party has met who might plausibly turn up in this region. None if there are none.
- **peak:** who or what stands at the act's climax.
  - **who:** one of the factions' leaders, from the rung that fits this act, or something that isn't a person when the act calls for it. In Act 4 it may be several designs coming to a head at once.
  - **why:** whose design blocks the party's way, and so why it's them. Choose by who is in the way, not by who the party likes.
  - **wants:** what they want right now.
  - **turns:** how they act as the climax goes on, the way a boss fight has turns: first, when pressed, when losing.
  - **where:** where in the region the climax happens.
- **hints:** two or three visible signs of the peak, each on its own: something a party would see or hear in passing, never the peak in person.

## How the acts rise
Tension, danger and grandeur climb from Act 1 to Act 4: the peak climbs the ladders, from a local face early to the top, or the factions' grandest designs, at the heart. Act 5 is the escape and the consequences: what the party did comes back at them. Never choose a peak who has already been one. After Act 5, an act follows what the player says they want to do.

Never make the region's danger a rule about where anyone can stand or what can be seen from where. Danger is who is there and what they do. Never build on anything in the worn-out list.
