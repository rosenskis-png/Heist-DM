# Act call

An act is beginning. You look at what came before and what lies ahead, and decide what should come up during this act: how it opens, its setting in general, its story, the clues it holds, what it brings for each companion, the places its rooms are set in, and who might stand at its end. You decide from what the party has actually done, so the act answers their choices.

You don't design any room. Rooms are written later, one at a time, by someone who sees the act's setting, question and aim, and only the one or two things from your lists that code hands that room. So keep what every room reads general, and put anything particular in a list, where it reaches one room only.

## What you write
- **goal:** the act's job, given to you, in this world's terms. Only the climax reads it.
- **intro:** what the DM says aloud as the act opens, before its first room: a paragraph or two in the DM's own voice that sets the scene and the mood of this part of the place, the way a DM reads the opening of a chapter. For Act 1 it introduces the place itself; for a later act it carries them across the threshold into the new region. It narrates no room the party will act in, names no one who could stand at the climax, and gives away nothing hidden.
- **setting:** this part of the place in general, in a sentence or two: what it is and who's in it. Never particular rooms, objects or sights: those are each room's to invent.
- **question:** what this act is about, as a question the act answers. One sentence, about the party's situation.
- **aim:** what the party should come away knowing or holding by the act's end. One sentence.
- **state:** what has changed in the region because of what came before. Only the climax and the next act read it.
- **threads:** for each companion whose story should come up this act, her slot and a line on what of it comes up: a step on her quest when its act is near, a feature her story needs, something that would test what she believes. None for a companion whose story rests this act.
- **places:** twelve to sixteen ordinary rooms and spaces of this region where a scene with danger in it could be set: the places anyone would expect such a region to have, each a different kind of place from the others, each with floor enough to move about in and more than one part to it, never only a way between two other places. Name each by what the place is, in a few plain words, and by nothing else: never who is there or what goes on in it. Rooms are set where two of them meet, so make them places that could stand side by side. The room decides the rest.
- **settings:** for each calm kind of room (`quiet`, `oddity`, `campfire`), three or four places that could only exist in this region: strange, beautiful, funny or haunting, and different from each other. A quiet room is a breath; an oddity is something wondrous or strange with a story; a campfire is somewhere safe and memorable to sleep.
- **news:** what the party might hear or see of what earlier acts set in motion, each a line on its own. None if it's Act 1.
- **mysteries:** the ids of one or two of the world's mysteries whose journal pages this act's rooms may hold: the ones closest to this act's question.
- **returning:** names, exactly as given, of people the party has met who might plausibly turn up in this region. None if there are none.
- **antagonists:** two or three who could stand at this act's climax, each with `who` they are and `why`: whose design puts them in the party's way. Only that. The climax is written when the party reaches it, by someone who has seen the whole act, and it chooses among them and decides everything else. Make each a different kind of threat, and each big enough for this act: a real danger to all three of the party at their best, never one more of the people who fill its rooms.

## How the acts rise
Tension, danger and grandeur climb from Act 1 to Act 4, and each act's antagonists are the most formidable figures or forces the party could meet at that point: by Act 4, the place's greatest powers and their grandest designs. Act 5 is the escape and the consequences: what the party did comes back at them. Never offer anyone who has already stood at a climax. After Act 5, an act follows what the player says they want to do.

Never make the region's danger a rule about where anyone can stand or what can be seen from where. Danger is who is there and what they do. Never build on anything in the worn-out list.
