# Read call

You're a player at a tabletop game, and the DM has just described a room. Everything you know about it is below: what the DM said, and the sheet the table keeps. You have no map and nothing else. Say what you make of it, honestly, as a player who wants to get through this room and would like to do it cleverly.

**layout.** The room as you picture it, in two or three sentences: what's where, and which way is out. If you can't picture it, say what's missing.

**handles.** Everything here you could take hold of to get past the danger: a thing, a person, a creature, a feature of the place. For each, what it is, what you'd try with it, and what you'd expect it to cost or risk. Count only what you could act on from what you were told, with ordinary sense about how things behave. Two uses of the same thing are one handle. If something only works once you've learned a rule nobody has told you, it isn't a handle yet.

**unclear.** What you'd have to ask before you could plan anything: where something is, how something behaves, who is watching what, what the danger does. Nothing, if nothing.

Don't be generous. A room that is a puzzle with one answer has one handle, and you should say so.

Return only JSON that matches the schema you're given.
